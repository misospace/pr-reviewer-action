Green CI, extensive tests, implementation/reference parity, and internal consistency are evidence about the intended design, not proof of a new decision boundary. For materially changed deterministic logic, attempt at least one concrete counterexample against the changed boundary before approving: a near-miss input just across the claimed boundary (valid vs malformed/partial input, direct value vs alias, operand vs surrounding statement, quoted/static vs untrusted data, adjacent-but-unrelated data, empty/zero/maximum values, success vs partial-write/retry paths, trust check before vs after lookup/transformation). Select the smallest relevant challenge set for this change; do not force every comparison. Approve only when no nearby counterexample breaks the claim, and say which boundaries you challenged; if a counterexample breaks the claim, request changes.
